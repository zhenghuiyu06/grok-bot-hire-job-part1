Work with Grok Bot

Create and manage Bots

View as Markdown

A Bot is a durable AI teammate with a name, a job, its own conversation, and working context that develops over time.

Give each Bot a clear job

Create a separate Bot when the work has a distinct:

  • Goal or area of ownership
  • Set of tools and sources
  • Working style
  • Approval boundary
  • Recurring schedule

Jobs like Talent Scout, Expense Manager, and Bug Reproduction give a Bot guidance and make its saved context reusable; a General Helper does neither.

Describe the role in operational terms:

Own the weekly account-health review. Pull product usage and support signals, flag evidence of churn or expansion, and produce a linked watch list for the customer-success team. Never contact a customer or change an account without approval.

Create a Bot

  1. Choose New Bot (the + button) at the top of the sidebar, or press Cmd/Ctrl+N. On a team, the + button opens a menu; choose Create a Bot.
  2. In Create New Bot, type a name, pick a shape and color for its picture, and choose Create.
  3. Grok Bot opens a chat with the new Bot.
  4. In your first message, give the Bot its job, sources, and approval boundaries, and ask it to remember them. Send a real first task with a clear finish line; see use cases for starter prompts.
  5. To rename the Bot, add a label, or change its picture, click the Bot's name at the top of the chat to open its details. See Change a Bot's name, picture, and label.

Your existing Bots can also suggest or create a focused Bot when a job should have a long-lived owner. Ask before creating several Bots if you want to keep the roster small.

Your primary Bot

Your primary Bot is your main point of contact. It checks in with you on its own and coordinates work with your other Bots.

If you don't have a primary Bot, Grok Bot shows Introducing your primary Bot. Choose Create primary Bot to add one, or choose Choose an existing Bot, select one of your Bots, and choose Confirm.

To pick a different one later, right-click your primary Bot in the sidebar and choose Replace with different Bot. On iPhone and Android, use Replace Primary Bot… in the Bot's menu. A shared Bot can't be your primary Bot.

Edit a Bot

When you discover a durable preference, boundary, or responsibility that should shape future work, tell the Bot and ask it to remember. Use ordinary messages for task-specific instructions:

  • Lasting rule: “From now on, never send external messages without approval.”
  • Task: “Draft follow-ups for these twelve accounts.”

The Bot keeps lasting rules in its memory. Personal Bots no longer have a description or a Bot settings page; to change what a Bot does, tell it in chat. A shared Bot still has a description, which its owner or a Manager edits under About in the Bot's details.

Change a Bot's name, picture, and label

Each Bot has its own name, picture, and label. Only the Bot's owner can change these; Rename Bot does not appear for a Bot you don't own.

To rename a Bot on desktop, right-click it in the sidebar and choose Rename Bot, or double-click its name in the sidebar. Type the new name and press Enter. If the sidebar is collapsed to icons, expand it first. You can also click the Bot's name at the top of the chat to open its details, then click the name to edit it. The label under the name works the same way; it shows Add a label when the Bot doesn't have one.

To change the picture on desktop, open the Bot's details and click its picture, then choose a tab:

  • Bot changes the look of the Bot's character.
  • Generate makes a picture from a short description. Describe the picture, choose Generate, then choose Set avatar.
  • Upload uses your own image. Drag in an image or choose Browse files, adjust it, then choose Set avatar. The image must be smaller than 25 MB.

On the phone, tap the Bot's name at the top of the chat to open its profile and tap Edit. Change the Name or the Title (the phone's name for the label) and tap Done. To change the picture, tap the picture or Avatar and choose Shape, Generate, or Upload. Upload offers Choose from Photos or Take Photo, then lets you crop the image; images can be up to 25 MB.

Organize your roster

  • Pin active Bots to keep them at the top of the sidebar.
  • Group Bots by project, client, or business with sections: right-click a Bot and choose Move to new section, or Move to once you have sections. Sections sync between desktop and iOS.
  • Hide from sidebar removes a Bot from the main list without deleting its work or pausing its routines. Hidden Bots stay active and keep their history.
  • To restore a hidden Bot, open Hidden Bots at the bottom of the sidebar and choose Show in sidebar from the Bot's menu. On iPhone and Android, choose Unhide in the hidden Bots list; when every Bot is hidden, the home screen shows Show Hidden Bots.

On iPhone, Duplicate a Bot to reuse a role for a different scope, like one account-health Bot per region. The copy carries the profile, settings, enabled skills, routines, and avatar, but not conversation history, learned memory, or attachments, so rename it and give it the new scope before assigning work.

Share a Bot

Share a template when someone else should start from the same Bot.

  1. Click the Bot's name at the top of the chat to open its details, choose Share, then choose Create a copy. The Bot builds a template and posts it in the chat.
  2. Choose Publish on the template card, then Copy link.
  3. Send the link. The recipient opens a preview on x.ai and can choose Add to Grok Bot. They need the Grok Bot app to finish adding it.

On iPhone and Android, open the Bot's menu and choose Share as Template.

After you publish, the same Share menu offers View template details, Update template to push your latest changes to the shared copy, and Delete Template to turn off the link.

A new template link is public. If your team allows only team-only sharing or has turned template sharing off, the app doesn't offer Create a copy, and publishing a template shows Your team doesn't allow public templates or Template sharing is turned off. Enterprise teams start with template sharing limited to the team; an admin can change it under Public template sharing.

A public link can be opened by anyone who has it and exposes the Bot's shared configuration: its identity, description, skills, and routines. A team-only link opens only for members of your team. Remove API keys, internal URLs, customer data, and anything else you would not put in a public document before you share.

Adding a shared template creates a copy on the recipient's account. They never get your computer, logins, or conversation history. To give your team one Bot to chat with instead of a copy each, share the Bot itself.

Shared templates are created by other users, not by Grok Bot. Adding one accepts the third-party bot terms shown at that point.

On the Teams plan and the Enterprise plan, admins control whether members can publish Bot templates publicly. See Public template sharing.

Add a Bot from a template

To browse Bot templates, open Connect apps in the sidebar and choose Bot templates. The gallery opens on x.ai in your browser. Open a template link and choose Import Bot to add your own copy of that Bot.

Delete a Bot

To delete a Bot, right-click it in the sidebar and choose Delete. This erases its profile, conversations, memory, routines, and attachments, including the shared facts it saved for your other Bots. It can't be undone. Files and sign-ins on the shared computer aren't isolated per Bot and can remain there; clean them up separately. Backend retention follows the applicable Cursor terms.

If you may need the work later, or want to keep the Bot's memory, choose Hide from sidebar instead.

What a Bot remembers

A Bot retains stable preferences, important facts, and summaries from its work, which lets it keep a role over time without replaying every prior message. It saves lasting things, such as:

  • How you like to work, and your preferences
  • Rules you ask it to keep, like “Always ask before you send an email.”
  • Facts about you, like your name and time zone
  • A dated log of what it has worked on with you

Logins, cards, and contact info, like addresses and phone numbers, belong in Vault, not in memory.

When a Bot saves something

A Bot decides on its own when something is worth keeping. It doesn't ask first. It often mentions a save in its reply, but not every save shows up in the chat. To make sure it keeps something, ask it to remember, for example: “Remember that I'm out every Friday.”

Across chats, Bots, and calls

What a Bot saves in one chat, it remembers in all your chats with it. Each Bot has its own memory, but a few facts about you, such as your name and time zone, are shared by all your Bots. When one Bot learns one of these, your other Bots know it too.

In a voice chat, a Bot can draw on your earlier calls and your recent chat with it. After you hang up, you can ask it about the call in chat.

In a group chat, your Bot can use what you told it in your private chat. If something should stay out of a group chat, tell your Bot. Each Bot in a group keeps its own memory; your Bot's memory isn't shared with the other Bots there, but everyone in the group can read what your Bot says.

See, correct, or remove a memory

There is no memory screen for a personal Bot. Ask in chat instead:

  • To see it: “What do you remember about me?”
  • To correct it: “That's out of date. I moved to Denver.”
  • To remove it: “Forget that I'm on the launch team.”

If a fact that all your Bots share is wrong, tell any of your Bots the right version. To remove a shared fact completely, ask the Bot that saved it.

What resets or deletes memory

What you doWhat happens to memory
Reset a conversationMemory is kept.
Delete a BotThat Bot's memory is deleted.
Delete your accountAll your Bots' memory is deleted.

When a message is blocked, the notice can offer Reset. If you choose it, you see Reset this conversation? The conversation history starts over, and the Bot's memory, files, routines, and settings are kept.

Who can see or change memory

A personal Bot's memory is yours, and only you can change it. A shared Bot can save things it learns for the whole team, and it tells you when it does. It doesn't save anything about you personally for the team; what it remembers about working with you stays between you and the Bot. A teammate's shared Bot doesn't get the facts your own Bots share about you.

Import memory from another assistant

To bring over what another AI assistant knows about you, open Settings → General and choose Start Import under Import. Copy the prompt into a chat with your other assistant, paste its answer into the second box, and choose Add to Memory. Grok Bot sends it to your primary Bot and opens that chat.

Memory is not a source of truth

Memory is not a substitute for an authoritative source:

  • Keep changing facts in the source system
  • Ask the Bot to cite or reopen current data for consequential decisions
  • Correct stale assumptions directly
  • Back safety boundaries with approvals and Auto-review rules rather than memory alone

Bots have separate roles and conversations, but they share the computer. They can pass context through direct messages, group chats, and shared files.

Organize a team of Bots

Start with the smallest useful roster:

  1. Give one Bot ownership of an end-to-end outcome.
  2. Add another Bot only when the work has a stable specialist role.
  3. Put Bots in a group chat when the handoff itself needs to be visible.
  4. Keep external actions behind a clear approval boundary.

For example, a Website Launch group might include a launch coordinator, a content editor, and an analytics reviewer. The coordinator can assign work, while the group preserves the handoffs in one conversation.
